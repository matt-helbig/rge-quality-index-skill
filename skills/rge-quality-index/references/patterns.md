# Cross-pillar patterns

Use when a borderline judgment needs a second look, or when a pillar feels wrong but no single deduction explains why. These are compound patterns — combinations that tend to travel together and are easy to miss one failure at a time.

They identify what to investigate. They do not assign bands, and they are not rejection rules. Score only the supported rubric deductions you can actually evidence, and count each root cause once.

## Patterns that warrant scrutiny

- **Density plus layout confusion.** Overwhelming content compounded by weak hierarchy and unclear CTA priority. Density alone is not the problem; density with no priority is.
- **Compounding legibility failures.** Thin weights on colored backgrounds, tight line heights, and inconsistent sizing together. Each is survivable; the combination is not.
- **Voiceless promotional copy.** Long CTAs, mission-speak, vague benefit stacking, and no discernible point of view. Describe the reader problem it creates. Never treat this as evidence of how the copy was produced.
- **All-image with no live text.** No live fallback, no progressive loading, essential message and action available only as pixels.
- **Structural Frankenstein.** Newsletter plus sale plus survey plus referral, with no hierarchy prioritizing any of them.
- **Strong desktop, failed mobile.** Poor stacking, insufficient tap targets, illegible text — a desktop-first execution that was never checked narrow.
- **Onboarding overwhelm.** Front-loaded information and company-focused copy, asking for commitment before the recipient has context.
- **Interaction confusion.** Non-clickable elements that look interactive, quiz patterns with no real outcome differentiation, affordances that promise what the email does not deliver.
- **Header clutter.** Multiple fonts, weights, colors, and treatments crammed above the fold, leaving no entry point.
- **Heavy animation with no fallback.** Several large GIFs, or a first frame that is blank or mid-transition, with no legible static state.
- **Bulletin-board layout.** Every item at equal visual priority — common in higher education, association, and internal comms sends. Nothing survives, because nothing was chosen.
- **Walls of centered small copy.** Extended centered body text with tight leading, which is measurably harder for many readers.

Do not reject on a count of emojis, bullets, fonts, languages, or familiar phrases. Neither AI authorship nor weak strategy can be inferred from those features.

## Patterns that often justify a feature

- **Works without images.** The core message survives images-off, because live text, alt text, and structure are each doing real work.
- **The inbox impression is fully crafted.** Sender name, subject, and preheader operate as one unit, and the opener pays off what they promised.
- **Unambiguous CTA hierarchy.** Color, size, and placement make primary and secondary instantly separable.
- **Storytelling with a payoff.** A real narrative — founder voice, partnership, behind-the-scenes — that uses the scroll to deliver something, not to show that it can scroll.
- **Authority content as the centerpiece.** Original research, benchmark data, or credible proof *is* the value proposition rather than decoration around an offer.
- **Recognizable without the logo.** Voice, palette, and structure identify the brand on their own.
- **Visible mobile-first thinking.** Stacking, tap targets, and reflow that were clearly designed narrow rather than adapted down.
- **Personalization that earns trust.** Genuine first-party signal — quiz result, purchase history, usage data, consultation context — responding to an evidenced need.
- **Dark mode handled deliberately.** Borders, outlines, and layered treatments preserve element definition instead of relying on background inversion.
- **Progressive enhancement with a real fallback.** Interactive elements that are content-appropriate and degrade cleanly.
- **Data visualization that earns its place.** Personalized or aggregate data made genuinely easier to understand for this recipient.
- **Live text where the category avoids it.** Rare in fashion and luxury especially; call it out when you see it.
- **Character-driven design at scale.** Mascot or illustration systems applied with visual discipline.
- **Typography as the concept.** Oversized serifs, expressive display, deliberate pacing — where the type is the idea rather than a container for it.
- **Exceptional restraint in a service message.** A transactional interaction so clear it is worth studying.

For any proposed feature, name the specific element, how it works, and the lesson another practitioner can use. A technique's presence is not a reason to feature it.
